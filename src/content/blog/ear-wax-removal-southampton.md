## What is earwax and why does it build up?

Earwax is a normal, healthy substance produced by the ear canal. It protects the ear by trapping dust and debris and has natural antibacterial properties. In most people, it clears on its own.

Build-up becomes a problem when wax doesn't clear as expected, often due to narrow ear canals, regular hearing aid or earbud use, or previous attempts to clean the ear with cotton buds, which tend to push wax further in rather than remove it.

## Earwax removal methods available in Southampton

Several methods exist for removing earwax, but not all are offered by every provider, and not all are equally suitable for every patient.

- **Microsuction** — a dry procedure using gentle, controlled suction under direct visualisation. This is the method we use for the great majority of patients, as it does not involve water and carries a lower risk profile than syringing.
- **Manual instrumentation** — removal of visible wax using fine instruments, sometimes used alongside microsuction.
- **Ear syringing** — an older, water-based method. We do not offer syringing, as it carries a higher risk of infection and eardrum injury than microsuction and has fallen out of favour clinically.

## What to expect at your appointment

Every appointment starts with an assessment, not an assumption that removal will go ahead. Your practitioner will ask about your symptoms and relevant history, including any ear pain, discharge, previous ear surgery, or perforated eardrum, before examining your ear canal to check whether wax is present and whether removal is appropriate.

If microsuction is suitable, it's carried out under direct visualisation using specialist equipment. You may hear suction noise and feel mild movement, but it should be explained clearly and you should be able to ask questions throughout. You'll be given aftercare advice afterwards, and if wax wasn't the cause of your symptoms, you'll be advised on next steps rather than left without an explanation.

## NHS vs. private earwax removal in Southampton

Most GP practices in Southampton, in line with national NHS guidance, no longer offer routine earwax removal as a funded service. This has left many patients — particularly hearing aid wearers, older adults, and those with recurrent wax build-up — without a straightforward NHS route for a common problem.

Private microsuction, delivered by a trained practitioner, is recognised by NICE as an appropriate first-line treatment for earwax removal. It gives Southampton patients a direct route to assessment and treatment without needing a GP referral.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need a GP referral to book?**
No. You can book directly for an assessment; a referral isn't required.

**How do I know if I need a clinic appointment or a home visit?**
Either is suitable for most patients. Home visits are worth considering if travel is difficult, or if you simply prefer to be seen at home.

**Will my earwax definitely be removed at my appointment?**
Not necessarily. Removal only goes ahead if it's assessed as appropriate. If wax isn't present or removal isn't suitable, you'll be advised accordingly.

**Is microsuction safe for hearing aid wearers?**
Microsuction is generally well suited to hearing aid wearers, as it's a dry procedure carried out under direct visualisation. Let your practitioner know you wear a hearing aid when you book.

**What should I do to prepare?**
You may be advised to use softening drops beforehand, depending on your circumstances. Avoid inserting cotton buds or other objects into the ear canal.
