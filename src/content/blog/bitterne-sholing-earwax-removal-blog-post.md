## Why Earwax Builds Up in the First Place

Earwax (cerumen) is a normal, healthy substance that protects and cleans the ear canal. For most people, it works its way out naturally. But a number of everyday habits can disrupt that process and lead to a build-up:

- **Cotton buds** — these push wax further into the canal rather than removing it
- **Earphones or hearing aids** — worn daily, they can block the natural migration of wax
- **Swimming** — regular exposure to water (the Sholing and Weston Shore areas have plenty of swimmers and paddlers in summer) can cause wax to swell
- **Naturally narrow or hairy ear canals** — some people are simply more prone to blockages

## Signs You May Need Professional Help

- A feeling of fullness or blockage in one or both ears
- Muffled hearing, especially noticeable in conversation or on the phone
- Tinnitus (ringing or buzzing) that seems to worsen
- Mild dizziness or an itchy, uncomfortable ear canal
- Hearing aid whistling or reduced performance, caused by wax build-up at the microphone or receiver

If any of this sounds familiar, it's worth getting checked rather than reaching for a cotton bud or an at-home wax removal kit, which can push wax deeper or, in rare cases, damage the eardrum.

## Microsuction vs. Ear Syringing

We primarily use **microsuction**, which is now considered the gold-standard method for earwax removal:

| | Microsuction | Ear Syringing (irrigation) |
|---|---|---|
| Method | Gentle suction under direct visualisation | Water flushed into the canal |
| Precision | High — clinician sees the canal throughout | Lower — no direct visualisation |
| Comfort | Dry, no water in the ear | Can feel uncomfortable, risk of vertigo |
| Suitability | Safe for perforated eardrums, grommets, chronic ear issues | Not suitable if eardrum is perforated |

Microsuction is quicker, more precise, and generally more comfortable — which is why it's become the preferred option across most private ear clinics in the UK, including ours.
